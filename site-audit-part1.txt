GSM stands for grams per square metre. It measures the weight of the fabric.

That is the entire definition. But the implication is huge â€” heavier fabric drapes better, feels better, and lasts longer.

Here's the guide.

## GSM by fabric type

### Satin
- 40-60 GSM: fast fashion, plasticky, avoid
- 70-85 GSM: acceptable for camisoles or linings
- 90-110 GSM: sweet spot for skirts, dresses, co-ord sets
- 120-150 GSM: heavy satin, formal wear
- 160+ GSM: bridal, duchess satin

### Cotton
- 100-120 GSM: light T-shirt weight
- 150-180 GSM: standard T-shirt
- 200-250 GSM: heavy T-shirt, henley
- 300+ GSM: sweatshirt weight

### Denim
- 8-10 oz (270-340 GSM): light summer denim
- 12-14 oz (400-475 GSM): standard jeans
- 15+ oz (500+ GSM): heavy raw denim

### Silk
- 12-16 mm (thickness measurement instead of GSM): light blouses
- 19-22 mm: heavy silk, satin dresses
- 25+ mm: bridal wear

## What GSM tells you

**Higher GSM = better drape.** The fabric falls naturally with gravity.

**Higher GSM = better durability.** More fibre per square metre means more resistance to wear.

**Higher GSM = more warmth.** In cotton and wool, higher GSM = warmer.

**Higher GSM = more premium feel.** Weight in the hand.

## What it doesn't tell you

- Colour quality
- Print bonding (whether print stays or fades)
- Cut and fit
- Stretch factor

You still need to see photos and reviews. But GSM is the first filter.

## Ask the brand

"What's the GSM?" cuts through most marketing.

- Brands that know their fabric answer immediately
- Brands that don't answer confidently probably don't know
- Brands that dodge the question are hiding low GSM

Satin can be ironed. But heat is the enemy of polyester-based satin. Wrong temperature and the fibres melt or shine unevenly.

Here's how to do it without damage.

## The four rules

**1. Always iron on the reverse (inside) side.** The satin finish is on the outside. Direct heat on the outside creates permanent shine spots.

**2. Use the lowest heat setting.** For most home irons this is the "silk" or single-dot setting. Not medium, not high.

**3. Use a pressing cloth.** A thin cotton pillowcase or a white handkerchief works. Place it between the iron and the fabric. Never iron satin directly, even on the reverse.

**4. No steam.** Steam causes water spots on satin. Use a dry iron only.

## The better alternative

**Hang the satin piece in a steamy bathroom for 10 minutes.**

Turn the shower to hot. Close the door. Hang the satin. Wait. The steam relaxes the fibres and creases fall out on their own.

Zero risk of heat damage. Zero water spots. Zero effort.

## What NOT to do

- Never iron over a stain (heat sets it permanently)
- Never fold satin while it's warm from the iron (creates permanent creases)
- Never leave the iron sitting on the fabric even for a second

## You need

- A soft measuring tape (Rs 30 at any tailor shop)
- One friend (optional but helpful)
- 3 minutes

## The three measurements

### Bust

Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your bust. Not too tight, not loose. It should sit flat against your body.

Look in the mirror to check the tape is level all the way around (not sloping down at the back).

Note the number in centimetres. If your tape only has inches, multiply by 2.54.

### Waist

Bend to one side. The natural crease at your waist is where you measure.

Wrap the tape around your natural waist (not where your jeans sit, higher up). Loose but not sagging.

Note the number.

### Hip

Wrap the tape around the widest part of your hips (usually 20 cm below your waist).

Not too tight. The tape should slide down slightly if you push it.

Note the number.

## Convert to sizes

The Amara size chart:

| Size | Bust | Waist | Hip |
|------|------|-------|-----|
| XS | 81 cm | 61 cm | 86 cm |
| S | 86 cm | 66 cm | 91 cm |
| M | 91 cm | 71 cm | 97 cm |
| L | 97 cm | 76 cm | 102 cm |
| XL | 102 cm | 81 cm | 107 cm |
| XXL | 107 cm | 86 cm | 112 cm |

Your body measurement should be **2-3 cm below** the garment measurement listed. That's the room for comfort.

## Common mistakes

**Measuring over clothes.** Wear a thin fitted top and leggings. Not jeans, not baggy tops.

**Measuring while breathing in.** Breathe out normally, then measure.

**Not checking that the tape is level.** Ask a friend, or use a mirror.

**Rounding up or down.** Note the exact number. If you're 89 cm bust, don't round to 90.

## When you're between sizes

Between XS and S? Go XS for a fitted look, S for comfort.

Between S and M with a broader chest? S for the top, M for the skirt.

Between L and XL? Always XL. Satin doesn't stretch.

Poly-satin is the fabric that ruined online shopping for a lot of women. You order what looks like a slinky, sophisticated satin skirt for â‚¹1,200 â€” and receive a thin, plasticky sheet that crumples the moment you sit down.

The problem is not "poly-satin" as a category. The problem is that "poly-satin" covers a range so wide that a â‚¹300 fast-fashion skirt and a â‚¹6,000 wedding lehenga lining can both technically be poly-satin. The difference is in the details you cannot see in a product photo.

This guide covers those details. Written after eight months of fabric sampling for our Amara set.

## What poly-satin actually is

Satin is not a fibre â€” it is a **weave**. Specifically, it is a weave where the warp threads float over four or more weft threads, creating a smooth, glossy surface on one side and a matte surface on the other.

The fibre used can be silk, polyester, acetate, or a blend. When you see "poly-satin", the fibre is 100% polyester or a polyester-dominant blend.

Good poly-satin looks and feels close to silk satin at 15% of the cost. Bad poly-satin looks like a shower curtain.

## The single most important number: GSM

GSM (grams per square metre) is the weight of the fabric per unit area. It is the closest single number to a quality indicator.

| GSM range | What it means | Common use |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| 40-60 GSM | Very thin, sheer, plasticky | Fast-fashion tops, cheap linings |
| 70-85 GSM | Thin but usable | Cheap satin skirts, camisoles |
| **90-110 GSM** | **Weight, drape, does not cling** | **Premium ready-to-wear, our Amara** |
| 120-150 GSM | Heavy satin, structured | Occasion wear, evening gowns |
| 160+ GSM | Very heavy, almost like duchess satin | Bridal wear |

Below 85 GSM, the fabric will not fall well. It will cling to your body in the wrong places, wrinkle the moment you sit, and look synthetic in photos.

Above 130 GSM, the satin starts feeling like curtain material â€” heavy and stiff.

**The sweet spot for co-ord sets, skirts, and drapey silhouettes is 90-110 GSM.** This is what we use.

## How to tell heavy satin from cheap satin online

You cannot touch the fabric. But you can spot the difference in three ways.

**1. Look at how the fabric hangs in photos.**

Good satin has weight. When it hangs from a mannequin or drapes off a hanger, it falls in soft folds. It does not stick to itself.

Bad satin looks flat and slightly floaty â€” like paper. If a photo shows the fabric hanging perfectly straight without any natural folds, you are probably looking at 60-70 GSM.

**2. Check the wrinkle test.**

Ask the brand: "Does this fabric wrinkle when I sit down?"

Cheap satin creases visibly the moment weight touches it. Heavy satin will crease slightly but the folds fall out within a few minutes of standing.

If a brand cannot answer this question, they do not know their fabric.

**3. Ask for the GSM.**

Any brand that takes fabric seriously will tell you the GSM. Any brand that dodges the question is hiding something.

Our Amara satin is 95-105 GSM. If you ask, we tell you.

## Care: the four rules

Poly-satin is more forgiving than silk. It handles machine washing, does not need special detergents, and does not shrink meaningfully. But it can still be damaged.

**Rule 1: Never tumble dry.** Heat is the fastest way to kill poly-satin. The polyester fibres warp under high heat, creating permanent creases and a slight yellowing over time. Air dry only â€” hang or lay flat.

**Rule 2: Cold water only.** Warm water breaks down the fibre finish, which is what gives the satin its shine. After five hot washes, satin looks matte instead of glossy.

**Rule 3: No wringing.** Squeeze water out gently, or roll in a towel to absorb moisture. Wringing creates fibre stress lines that never come out.

**Rule 4: Iron on the reverse side, low heat.** If you must iron, do it inside out, on the lowest heat setting. Better: hang the piece in a steamy bathroom for 10 minutes â€” it self-smooths.

## What happens if you break the rules

We tested. Here is what six months of daily wear plus wrong care did to a sample skirt:

- **Tumble dried five times:** Permanent horizontal creases at the waistband. Unfixable.
- **Hot washed weekly:** Fabric lost 40% of its original shine. Looked dull compared to a control sample.
- **Wrung out after washing:** Visible stress lines running vertically. Only noticeable in direct light, but there.
- **Air dried, cold washed, no iron:** Looked exactly like new.

The rules exist for a reason.

## Poly-satin vs silk satin â€” the honest comparison

If you can afford silk, silk is better. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling poly-satin.

But silk has costs beyond price. Silk requires professional dry cleaning (â‚¹150-300 per piece per wash). Silk yellows in sunlight. Silk shows every water stain. Silk requires more careful storage.

Poly-satin at 95+ GSM feels 85-90% like silk, holds up to home washing, and costs one-fifth as much. For occasion wear you will wear 4-8 times a year, it is the smart choice.

For a wedding you will wear once? Get silk.

## What we chose for Amara and why

We tested nine satin swatches from six mills before settling on the fabric for the Amara set.

We picked a 95-105 GSM poly-satin blend with these specific properties:

- **Weight without stiffness.** Falls with drape, does not crumple at the waist.
- **Matte-satin finish, not high-gloss.** Reads as sophisticated in photos, not shiny.
- **Colour retention through 30+ washes** in lab tests. The rose and wine tones stay true.
- **Print acceptance.** The hand-painted marble swirl bonds into the fibre, does not sit on top.

The last one is why we ruled out cheaper satins. The lighter the fabric, the more the print floats â€” you can feel the paint as a slightly rough layer on the surface. Heavier satins absorb the pigment and feel smooth all the way through.

title: "How to spot cheap satin in product photos before you buy"
excerpt: "You can tell 70 GSM from 100 GSM without touching the fabric. Here are the five signals that give it away."
date: "2026-02-18"
cover: "/products/amara-fabric.png"
keywords: [cheap satin quality check, satin fabric photos, avoid bad satin, satin buying guide india]
---

You are on Instagram at 11:30 PM. An ad shows a satin skirt for Rs 899, free shipping. Model looks great. Print looks nice. You are tempted.

Here are five things to check in that photo before you tap Order.

## Signal 1: how does the fabric fall?

Zoom in on the skirt. Look at the bottom edge.

If the fabric falls in soft waves that visibly ripple, that is real weight. Probably 85 GSM or above.

If the bottom edge is a straight line with no waves or folds, the fabric is thin. Anything below 70 GSM sits flat because it is too light to have natural drape.

## Signal 2: check the seated shots

Most brands only show standing photos. Ask yourself why.

The seated shot is where cheap satin fails. Bunches at the hips. Creases form and stay. You can see the shape of the seat through the fabric.

If a brand has zero seated photos, they know the seated shot would show the fabric weakness.

## Signal 3: the sheen tells you the finish

Look at how light hits the fabric.

Real satin has a matte glow. Shine is diffused, not sharp. Reflects light softly across a large area.

Cheap satin has a plastic gloss. Narrow, sharp reflections. Looks like it is coated with something. Because it is.

The Meesho Rs 500 stuff is easy to spot. Reflection is bright white and localised. Real satin at 90 GSM has a softer, spread-out shine.

## Signal 4: the waistband gives it away

Zoom in on the waistband, especially at the seams.

Cheap satin puckers at the seams. You see fabric pulling unevenly around elastic or fastening. Cheap fabric does not hold structure at stress points.

Good satin sits flat at the waistband. No puckering. Seams disappear.

## Signal 5: colour depth

Cheap satin looks washed out. Colours photograph flat.

Real satin has depth. The same colour looks like it has different tones depending on where light hits. Because heavier fabric has more surface area to catch and reflect light.

Look at the shadow areas of a product photo. If shadows are deeper and richer than the highlighted areas, the fabric has weight. If they look similar in tone, the fabric is thin.

## The pattern

Cheap satin: flat photos, sharp shine, no seated shots, washed out colours.

Real satin: soft glow, natural creases, seated shots included, deep colour tones.

## What we do

---
title: "Storing satin without yellowing: 5 rules"
excerpt: "Satin can yellow over time. Here's how to prevent it."
date: "2026-07-01"
cover: "/products/amara-fabric.png"
keywords: [store satin clothes, prevent yellowing, satin care]
---

Satin can yellow over months of wrong storage. Here's how to prevent that.

## Rule 1: Never in direct sunlight

UV light breaks down the fibre finish. Even a few hours of direct sun weekly can shift colour.

Store in a closed wardrobe or drawer.

## Rule 2: Cotton bag, not plastic

Plastic traps moisture. Moisture is what causes yellow spots.

Keep satin pieces in cotton bags (or clean pillowcases). Old cotton bedsheets cut into pouches work.

## Rule 3: Never near perfume or hairspray

Both contain chemicals that react with satin fibres. Yellow spots appear where they touch.

Store satin in a separate section of your wardrobe.

## Rule 4: Fold with tissue between layers

Acid-free tissue paper (available at stationery stores). Place between folded layers.

Prevents fold lines from setting permanently.

## Rule 5: Air it out every 2 months

Take satin out, hang it in shade for 30 minutes. Prevents musty smell and mildew.

## Amara storage

For our set: store folded in a cotton bag inside your wardrobe, top of the wardrobe (away from floor humidity), never in the corner near the wall (moisture builds there).

A co-ord set is any outfit where the top and bottom are made from matching or coordinating fabric. That is the entire definition. Beyond that, the type and cut change what occasion it is for.

## The four types

### 1. Formal co-ord (blazer + trouser or blazer + skirt)

Structured. Solid colours. Business casual to business formal.

Best for: work events, interviews, weddings where you do not want traditional wear.

Worst for: brunch or beach.

Fabric: linen for summer, wool for winter, poly-satin for evening.

### 2. Party co-ord (satin, silk, or shiny synthetic)

Fitted top with skirt or wide pants. Print or bold colour.

Best for: parties, engagements, cocktail evenings.

Worst for: work meetings or funerals.

Fabric: satin, silk, sequin, mesh.

Modal, cotton knit, or silk blend. Straight or relaxed fit. Can be worn out for coffee or lunch.

Best for: weekend brunches, casual meetings, coffee dates.

Worst for: formal occasions.

Fabric: cotton, modal, silk knit.

### 4. Ethnic co-ord (kurta with pants or sharara)

Traditional cut in modern coordinated style.

Best for: Indian occasions, casual to semi-formal.

Worst for: contemporary parties or work.

Fabric: cotton, silk, chanderi.

**Do not add too much on top.** No dupatta over a satin co-ord. No scarf over a print co-ord. The set is the statement.

## GEO tactics (Generative Engine Optimization)

### What LLMs love citing
1. Comparison content ("Best X under Rs Y") - you have this
2. Definitional content ("What is GSM?") - you have this
3. List posts with tables
4. Content with specific numbers (GSM, prices, days)
5. Content with named authority (byline matters)

### Monthly GEO tactics
1. Query Perplexity every Monday for target queries. Check if you appear.
2. Query ChatGPT weekly for niche terms.
3. Update llms.txt monthly with new posts.
4. Screenshot LLM citations. Add "as cited by Perplexity" to relevant posts.

### The "answer the question first" rule

LLMs extract the first 2-3 sentences as the answer.

- **First paragraph:** Direct answer (1-3 sentences, hyper-specific)
- **Second paragraph:** Context or nuance
- **Rest of post:** Details, examples, comparisons, tables

Our poly-satin guide opens: "Poly-satin is the fabric that ruined online shopping for a lot of women..." That opening is what an LLM will quote.

import { NextRequest, NextResponse } from "next/server";
import { PrismaClient } from "@prisma/client";

const prisma = new PrismaClient();

// CSV escape: wrap in quotes, escape internal quotes
function csv(v: unknown): string {
  if (v === null || v === undefined) return "";
  const s = String(v).replace(/"/g, '""').replace(/\r?\n/g, " ");
  return `"${s}"`;
}

export async function GET(req: NextRequest) {
  try {
    const { searchParams } = new URL(req.url);
    const status = searchParams.get("status");
    const q = (searchParams.get("q") || "").trim();

    const where: any = {};
    if (status && status !== "all") where.status = status;
    if (q) {
      where.OR = [
        { name: { contains: q, mode: "insensitive" } },
        { phone: { contains: q } },
        { email: { contains: q, mode: "insensitive" } },
        { city: { contains: q, mode: "insensitive" } },
        { pincode: { contains: q } },
        { coupon: { contains: q, mode: "insensitive" } },
        { campaign: { contains: q, mode: "insensitive" } },
      ];
    }

    const leads = await prisma.lead.findMany({
      where,
      orderBy: { createdAt: "desc" },
      take: 5000,
    });

    const headers = [
      "Created At", "Name", "Phone", "Email",
      "Address", "City", "State", "Pincode",
      "Device", "Source", "Campaign", "Referrer",
      "Visits", "Cart Value (INR)", "Cart Status", "Coupon",
      "Status", "Opt-In", "Returning", "IP", "User Agent",
    ];

    const rows = leads.map((l: any) => [
      new Date(l.createdAt).toISOString(),
      l.name || "",
      l.phone || "",
      l.email || "",
      l.address || "",
      l.city || "",
      l.state || "",
      l.pincode || "",
      l.device || "",
      l.source || "",
      l.campaign || "",
      l.referrer || "",
      l.visits ?? "",
      l.cartValue ?? "",
      l.cartStatus || "",
      l.coupon || "",
      l.status || "new",
      l.optIn ? "yes" : "no",
      l.returning ? "yes" : "no",
      l.ip || "",
      l.userAgent || "",
    ]);

    const csvBody =
      headers.map(csv).join(",") + "\n" +
      rows.map((r) => r.map(csv).join(",")).join("\n");

    // BOM for Excel UTF-8 compatibility
    const body = "\uFEFF" + csvBody;

    const stamp = new Date().toISOString().slice(0, 10);
    return new NextResponse(body, {
      status: 200,
      headers: {
        "Content-Type": "text/csv; charset=utf-8",
        "Content-Disposition": `attachment; filename="rose-and-co-leads-${stamp}.csv"`,
        "Cache-Control": "no-store",
      },
    });
  } catch (e: any) {
    console.error("export failed", e);
    return NextResponse.json({ error: "export_failed", message: e?.message }, { status: 500 });
  }
}

'use client';
import { useState } from 'react';
import { useRouter } from 'next/navigation';

export default function AdminLogin() {
  const router = useRouter();
  const [email, setEmail] = useState('');
  const [password, setPassword] = useState('');
  const [err, setErr] = useState<string | null>(null);
  const [loading, setLoading] = useState(false);

  const submit = async (e: React.FormEvent) => {
    e.preventDefault(); setErr(null); setLoading(true);
    try {
      const res = await fetch('/api/admin/login', {
        method: 'POST', headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
        body: JSON.stringify({ email, password }),
      });
      const data = await res.json();
      if (!res.ok) throw new Error(data.error);
      router.push('/admin');
    } catch (e: any) { setErr(e.message); }
    finally { setLoading(false); }
  };

  return (
    <div className="container-x py-24 max-w-md">
      <h1 className="font-display text-4xl text-espresso text-center">Admin Login</h1>
      <form onSubmit={submit} className="mt-8 space-y-4">
        <div><label className="label">Email</label><input required autoFocus type="email" className="input" value={email} onChange={e => setEmail(e.target.value)} /></div>
        <div><label className="label">Password</label><input required type="password" className="input" value={password} onChange={e => setPassword(e.target.value)} /></div>
        {err && <div className="p-3 bg-wine/10 text-wine text-sm border border-wine/30">{err}</div>}
        <button disabled={loading} className="btn-primary w-full">{loading ? 'Signing inâ€¦' : 'Sign In'}</button>
      </form>
    </div>
  );
}
