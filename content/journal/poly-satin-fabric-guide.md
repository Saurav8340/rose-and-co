Poly-satin is the fabric that ruined online shopping for a lot of women. You order what looks like a slinky, sophisticated satin skirt for ₹1,200 — and receive a thin, plasticky sheet that crumples the moment you sit down.

The problem is not "poly-satin" as a category. The problem is that "poly-satin" covers a range so wide that a ₹300 fast-fashion skirt and a ₹6,000 wedding lehenga lining can both technically be poly-satin. The difference is in the details you cannot see in a product photo.

This guide covers those details. Written after eight months of fabric sampling for our Amara set.

## What poly-satin actually is

Satin is not a fibre — it is a **weave**. Specifically, it is a weave where the warp threads float over four or more weft threads, creating a smooth, glossy surface on one side and a matte surface on the other.

The fibre used can be silk, polyester, acetate, or a blend. When you see "poly-satin", the fibre is 100% polyester or a polyester-dominant blend.

Good poly-satin looks and feels close to silk satin at 15% of the cost. Bad poly-satin looks like a shower curtain.

## The single most important number: GSM

GSM (grams per square metre) is the weight of the fabric per unit area. It is the closest single number to a quality indicator.

| GSM range | What it means | Common use |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| 40-60 GSM | Very thin, sheer, plasticky | Fast-fashion tops, cheap linings |
| 70-85 GSM | Thin but usable | Cheap satin skirts, camisoles |
| **90-110 GSM** | **Weight, drape, does not cling** | **Premium ready-to-wear, our Amara** |
| 120-150 GSM | Heavy satin, structured | Occasion wear, evening gowns |
| 160+ GSM | Very heavy, almost like duchess satin | Bridal wear |

Below 85 GSM, the fabric will not fall well. It will cling to your body in the wrong places, wrinkle the moment you sit, and look synthetic in photos.

Above 130 GSM, the satin starts feeling like curtain material — heavy and stiff.

**The sweet spot for co-ord sets, skirts, and drapey silhouettes is 90-110 GSM.** This is what we use.

## How to tell heavy satin from cheap satin online

You cannot touch the fabric. But you can spot the difference in three ways.

**1. Look at how the fabric hangs in photos.**

Good satin has weight. When it hangs from a mannequin or drapes off a hanger, it falls in soft folds. It does not stick to itself.

Bad satin looks flat and slightly floaty — like paper. If a photo shows the fabric hanging perfectly straight without any natural folds, you are probably looking at 60-70 GSM.

**2. Check the wrinkle test.**

Ask the brand: "Does this fabric wrinkle when I sit down?"

Cheap satin creases visibly the moment weight touches it. Heavy satin will crease slightly but the folds fall out within a few minutes of standing.

If a brand cannot answer this question, they do not know their fabric.

**3. Ask for the GSM.**

Any brand that takes fabric seriously will tell you the GSM. Any brand that dodges the question is hiding something.

Our Amara satin is 95-105 GSM. If you ask, we tell you.

## Care: the four rules

Poly-satin is more forgiving than silk. It handles machine washing, does not need special detergents, and does not shrink meaningfully. But it can still be damaged.

**Rule 1: Never tumble dry.** Heat is the fastest way to kill poly-satin. The polyester fibres warp under high heat, creating permanent creases and a slight yellowing over time. Air dry only — hang or lay flat.

**Rule 2: Cold water only.** Warm water breaks down the fibre finish, which is what gives the satin its shine. After five hot washes, satin looks matte instead of glossy.

**Rule 3: No wringing.** Squeeze water out gently, or roll in a towel to absorb moisture. Wringing creates fibre stress lines that never come out.

**Rule 4: Iron on the reverse side, low heat.** If you must iron, do it inside out, on the lowest heat setting. Better: hang the piece in a steamy bathroom for 10 minutes — it self-smooths.

## What happens if you break the rules

We tested. Here is what six months of daily wear plus wrong care did to a sample skirt:

- **Tumble dried five times:** Permanent horizontal creases at the waistband. Unfixable.
- **Hot washed weekly:** Fabric lost 40% of its original shine. Looked dull compared to a control sample.
- **Wrung out after washing:** Visible stress lines running vertically. Only noticeable in direct light, but there.
- **Air dried, cold washed, no iron:** Looked exactly like new.

The rules exist for a reason.

## Poly-satin vs silk satin — the honest comparison

If you can afford silk, silk is better. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling poly-satin.

But silk has costs beyond price. Silk requires professional dry cleaning (₹150-300 per piece per wash). Silk yellows in sunlight. Silk shows every water stain. Silk requires more careful storage.

Poly-satin at 95+ GSM feels 85-90% like silk, holds up to home washing, and costs one-fifth as much. For occasion wear you will wear 4-8 times a year, it is the smart choice.

For a wedding you will wear once? Get silk.

## What we chose for Amara and why

We tested nine satin swatches from six mills before settling on the fabric for the Amara set.

We picked a 95-105 GSM poly-satin blend with these specific properties:

- **Weight without stiffness.** Falls with drape, does not crumple at the waist.
- **Matte-satin finish, not high-gloss.** Reads as sophisticated in photos, not shiny.
- **Colour retention through 30+ washes** in lab tests. The rose and wine tones stay true.
- **Print acceptance.** The hand-painted marble swirl bonds into the fibre, does not sit on top.

The last one is why we ruled out cheaper satins. The lighter the fabric, the more the print floats — you can feel the paint as a slightly rough layer on the surface. Heavier satins absorb the pigment and feel smooth all the way through.
