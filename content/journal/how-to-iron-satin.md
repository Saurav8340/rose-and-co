Satin can be ironed. But heat is the enemy of polyester-based satin. Wrong temperature and the fibres melt or shine unevenly.

Here's how to do it without damage.

## The four rules

**1. Always iron on the reverse (inside) side.** The satin finish is on the outside. Direct heat on the outside creates permanent shine spots.

**2. Use the lowest heat setting.** For most home irons this is the "silk" or single-dot setting. Not medium, not high.

**3. Use a pressing cloth.** A thin cotton pillowcase or a white handkerchief works. Place it between the iron and the fabric. Never iron satin directly, even on the reverse.

**4. No steam.** Steam causes water spots on satin. Use a dry iron only.

## The better alternative

**Hang the satin piece in a steamy bathroom for 10 minutes.**

Turn the shower to hot. Close the door. Hang the satin. Wait. The steam relaxes the fibres and creases fall out on their own.

Zero risk of heat damage. Zero water spots. Zero effort.

## What NOT to do

- Never iron over a stain (heat sets it permanently)
- Never fold satin while it's warm from the iron (creates permanent creases)
- Never leave the iron sitting on the fabric even for a second
