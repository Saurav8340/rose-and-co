Diwali has become synonymous with kurta sets. That's fine. But if you want to break from tradition without ditching the occasion, here are five outfit types that work.

## 1. Satin co-ord in warm tones

Rose, wine, burnt orange, deep gold. The Amara marble swirl works.

Wear with: gold jhumkas, gold bangles (2-3), block heels in gold or wine.

## 2. Silk saree draped as a co-ord

Take a plain silk saree, drape it as a pre-stitched skirt with the pallu tied at the waist. Wear a fitted crop top on top.

Modern, still uses the silk you'd usually wear.

## 3. Metallic slip dress + kaftan cover-up

Gold, bronze, or copper slip dress with a lightweight cover-up over. For house parties, not pujas.

## 4. Structured jumpsuit in Diwali colours

Deep green, wine, burnt orange. Structured, comfortable, dance-friendly.

## 5. Anarkali with contemporary silhouette

Modern Anarkali cut (straight sleeves, clean neckline). Feels traditional without being generic.

## What NOT to wear on Diwali

- Anything black (too western for the occasion in most Indian families)
- Anything very casual (jeans, sneakers)
- Anything that clashes with warm interior lighting (Diwali is lit differently than restaurants)
